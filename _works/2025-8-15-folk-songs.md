<blockquote>
<p>I have always sensed a profound uneasiness while listening to popular songs performed with piano accompaniment. This is one of the reasons why, in 1964, I wrote <i>Folk Songs</i> - a tribute to the artistry and the vocal intelligence of Cathy Berberian. This work exists in two versions: one for voice and seven players (flute/piccolo, clarinet, two percussions, harp, viola, cello), the other for voice and orchestra (1973).</p>
<p>It is an anthology of eleven folk songs of various origins (United States, Armenia, France, Sicily, Sardinia, etc.), chosen from old records, printed anthologies, or heard sung from folk musicians and friends. I have given the songs a new rhythmic and harmonic interpretation: in a way, I have recomposed them. The instrumental part has an important function: it is meant to underline and comment on the expressive and cultural roots of each song. Such roots signify not only the ethnic origins of the songs but also the history of the authentic uses that have been made of them.</p>
<p>Two of the eleven songs (“La donna ideale” and “Ballo”) are only intentionally popular: I composed them myself in 1947 to anonymous Genoese and Sicilian texts.</p>
<cite>Luciano Berio</cite>
</blockquote>

My primary source for this arrangement is the version for voice and seven players. I stay true to Berio’s pitches and rhythms in all but a handful of cases: the independent tempo material in movements 1 and 10, which is written out as tuplets in the original tempo for ease of coordination; and movements 5 and 8, which employ extended piano techniques to capture the feeling, rather than the exact notes, of the original. _— Kian Ravaei_
